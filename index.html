Evidence to claim: one trial, three audiences, and a pre-check before anything reaches review

Jane Natividad  ·  jane-natividad.github.io  ·  View on GitHub

Strong evidence still has to be written differently for a clinician, a patient, and a payer — and what a product is legally allowed to say depends on what kind of product it is. This prototype drafts an audience-specific claim from the trial endpoints you pick, then runs it through a transparent rule-based pre-check: non-significant endpoints, missing citations, out-of-population extrapolation, superlatives, and efficacy statistics in channels where the product category doesn't allow them. It doesn't replace Medical, Legal and Regulatory review. It makes the first draft worth their time.

Design choices
1
One evidence base, several compliant claimsThe same result is a cited statistic for a clinician, a purpose-and-mechanism statement for a patient, and an outcomes-and-tolerability story for a payer.
2
The product category sets the rulesA prescription medicine, a medical food, and a digital therapeutic sit under different claim rules. In many markets a food for special medical purposes can state what it's for, but can't carry a promotional efficacy number on pack or in patient materials.
3
Every rule is visibleNo model decides what's compliant. Each check is a plain rule you can read in the code, and every flag says which rule fired.
Prototype with fictional data. The product, both trials, and every figure are invented for demonstration. The rules are simplified illustrations of common claim principles, not legal or regulatory advice for any market.
